PIRATE LIFE MATEY

Ahoy, ye scurvy dog! Ye want to know what it be like to sail the briny deep in the 12th century? Well, gather 'round the capstan and let me spin ye a yarn about the life of a sea rover back in the days of kings and castles.

The Good Stuff (The Plunder)

  • Riches Beyond Yer Wildest Dreams: If ye manage to raid a merchant vessel or a coastal village, yer pockets could be heavy with gold, silver, and spices. No more slaving away in the fields for a pittance!
  • Freedom from the Lord's Yoke: On the high seas, no king nor lord can tell ye what to do. Ye answer only to yer captain and the crew. It be a democratic sort of hell, where every man gets a say (mostly).
  • Adventure at Every Turn: From the stormy North Sea to the sunny Mediterranean, every day brings a new horizon. Ye'll see lands most folk only dream of in their taverns.
  • Brotherhood of the Coast: Pirates look out fer each other. Once ye join the crew, ye're family. We share the loot, we share the grog, and we share the fight.

The Bad Stuff (The Curse)

  • Disease and Death: Scurvy will rot yer teeth and make yer gums bleed. Dysentery will leave ye weak as a kitten. And if ye get a cut? A simple splinter can kill ye faster than a cannonball.
  • Brutal Punishments: If ye steal from the crew or disobey orders, it's keelhauling or marooning fer ye. Leave ye on a deserted island with nothing but a pistol and a bottle o' gin (well, maybe just a knife and some hardtack).
  • Hanging is Guaranteed: Unlike later centuries, there were no pardons for pirates in the 12th century. If the king's navy catches ye, it's the gallows without trial. No last words, no mercy.
  • Hardship at Sea: Sleeping on damp decks, eating weevil-infested hardtack, and working until yer hands bleed. The sea don't care if ye're tired or sick. She'll take ye if she wants.
Always be yourself, unless you can be a pirate, then be a pirate.
